TYWE2L-2-THREAD carrier schematic

An ESP32-H2-MINI-1-H4S on a carrier board that drops into a Tuya TYWE2L footprint, converting a Wi-Fi Tuya device to Matter over Thread. Seven legs face the host: five GPIOs plus GND and 3V3. Drawn by hand from the KiCad source, with every net traced from the sheet rather than from the notes on it.

Legend

  • Wire and junction. A dot marks a connected node.
  • Fitted component. Solid outline, designator and value as the schematic has them.
  • DNP, not fitted. Dashed outline and grey text: the footprint exists, the part does not.
  • Safety-critical. R4 only. Warm outline plus a triangle mark.
  • Strapping pin. Latched at reset into GPIO_STRAP_REG. Never load one.
  • Rear-face test pad. All fifteen pads sit on the back of the carrier.
  • Naming crossover. Where an ESP8285 leg number and an H2 pin number collide.
  • Power flags. GND and +3V3 symbols name the rail, exactly as on the sheet.

Notes and constraints

VBAT must never be fed separately

Module pin 15 is left unconnected and carries a no-connect flag, so VBAT stays on the module internal 3V3 tie. H2 GPIO12, GPIO13 and GPIO14, which are legs 3, 1 and 2, sit on the VBAT / VDDA_PMU domain, and logic thresholds are referred to whichever domain the pin sits on. Feed VBAT from a battery and three of the five legs take different thresholds and different output swings from the other two, on the same host connector, with nothing to warn anybody. This is a constraint, not a default.

The leg hole diameter is an assumption

The 0.9 mm hole on a 1.4 mm pad in J1 is a guess, not a measurement. Tuya never publishes the leg diameter. Every routing-channel figure scales off it: a 1.0 mm hole with a 1.5 mm pad drops the leg-to-module channel from 0.750 to 0.700 mm. Caliper a physical TYWE2L before ordering boards. This is a fabrication blocker.

The antenna keepout sets the height

The keepout is the top 5.4 mm of the module across its full 13.2 mm width, with no FR4 and no copper under it. That caps the carrier at 13.6 mm tall and pushes the assembly to 19.0 mm, against 17.272 mm for the TYWE2L it replaces. The stack thickness still matches at 0.6 mm PCB plus 2.4 mm module.

R4 pulls GPIO25 up, never down

GPIO25 has no internal pull resistor and its strapping value must be set externally, so it cannot be left floating. A 0 on GPIO25 selects the JTAG pads MTDI, MTCK, MTMS and MTDO. Under pin mapping v2 none of those four is a host line, so R4 can no longer seize a host net, but the direction stays fixed. Do not tidy it to a pull-down.

The links are fitted, the pull-downs are not

R10 to R14 are 0R 0402 links fitted by default. Remove one to isolate a contentious host net during bring-up. R20 to R24 are 10k pull-downs sitting on the host side of those links, not fitted, kept as optional insurance for an unknown host. Nothing on the carrier side depends on them: all five v2 host pins come out of reset with input disabled, output disabled and no pull.

No leg touches a strapping pin

GPIO_STRAP_REG latches GPIO2, GPIO3, GPIO8, GPIO9 and GPIO25. All five v2 host lines avoid the lot, so nothing a host device does to a leg can change boot mode. GPIO2 and GPIO3 remain reachable on spare pads TP12 and TP13, which is why those two are marked. GPIO4 on TP14 carries an after-reset pull-up and is vetoed as a host line outright.
